# SGL compile speed

368 SGL translations, 26K non-blank lines, each compiled on its own to target text, in one process on one thread.
Every number is the fastest of 10 runs, summed over the corpus, the shader compiler survey's method.
Machine: AMD Ryzen 9 5900X 12-Core Processor, Windows 11.

The first compile in a process also checks the prelude, once: 22.4ms in all, which no row includes.

| path | compiled | total | median per shader | slowest shader |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SGL → hlsl-dx12 | 368 | 338ms | 328µs | 22.7ms `fs_s2h_gather` |
| SGL → hlsl-vulkan | 368 | 335ms | 321µs | 22.0ms `fs_s2h_gather` |
| SGL → wgsl | 367 | 324ms | 282µs | 20.5ms `fs_s2h_gather` |
| SGL → msl | 368 | 371ms | 394µs | 22.0ms `fs_s2h_gather` |

Not compiled on every target: `bgfx/shaders/52-layered/vs_layered`; `sgl-coverage.py` says whether it failed or requires a device feature the target lacks.

## Against the survey

The survey ran on a Ryzen 9 7950X, this run on a 5900X, so its numbers are scaled by what Naga 30.0.1, its own version, takes here.
Naga on the bgfx WGSL of this corpus, timed the same way in a Rust harness, takes 328ms to SPIR-V where the survey has 221ms: a factor of 1.48.
That one factor stands in for a whole machine and corpus difference, so the scaled rows are estimates, good to the first digit.

| path | input | total here | median per shader |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SGL → HLSL, WGSL or MSL** | SGL, 26K lines | **0.32-0.37s, measured** | 280-390µs |
| Naga, WGSL → SPIR-V, HLSL or MSL | WGSL, 44K lines | 0.33s, measured | 126-136µs |
| Tint, WGSL → SPIR-V, MSL or HLSL | WGSL | ~0.9-1.1s, scaled | ~0.6ms |
| Glslang+SPIRV-Tools, HLSL → SPIR-V | HLSL, 320K lines | ~7.3s, scaled | ~5ms |
| DXC, HLSL → DXIL | HLSL | ~8.9s, scaled | ~17ms |
| Slang, HLSL → HLSL, WGSL or MSL | HLSL | ~20s, scaled | ~48ms |
| FXC, HLSL → DXBC | HLSL | ~23s, scaled | ~7ms |

What the rows do not share:

- **SGL and Naga's distributions differ.** Naga is 2.9 times faster on the median shader, and SGL two to four times faster on the large S2H shaders, which hold a quarter of SGL's total.
  A trivial shader costs SGL at least 115µs and Naga 18µs: SGL pays a fixed cost per compile that Naga does not.
- **Neither SGL nor Naga nor Tint optimizes.** SGL's HLSL and MSL still go through DXC or Metal before a driver takes them, so SGL to DXIL is this row plus DXC on SGL's output, which is not measured here.
- **HLSL rows parse a thousand lines of bgfx helpers per shader**, which is why HLSL is ten times the size of the rest; SGL checks its prelude once per process instead.
